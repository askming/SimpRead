# Ranking California high schools: Private schools that perform best on UC admissions

_Editor’s note: This story has been updated with corrected numbers on the share of students admitted to UC campuses from private schools across California. An earlier version of the story underestimated those numbers by about half due to a calculation error. The ranking of private schools presented in the story was not substantially changed by this correction._

California’s private high schools vary substantially in terms of the share of students admitted to University of California campuses. While two-thirds of students from one school received a UC admission in the past couple years, in many schools less than 20% of seniors were admitted.

To get admission rates to UCs for more than 240 private high schools in California, the Chronicle combined 2023-24 school year data from [the ](https://www.cde.ca.gov/ds/si/ps/)[California Department of Education](https://www.cde.ca.gov/ds/si/ps/) with admissions data from the [University of California](https://www.universityofcalifornia.edu/about-us/information-center/admissions-source-school). The table below shows the admission rates for each school with at least 30 seniors total, a cutoff meant to reduce volatility from very small graduating classes. Select a county to see schools near you.

#### UC admissions rate at California private high schools

Data for seniors who graduated in 2024. Application and admission rates are calculated as the number of students who applied and were admitted to any UC divided by the total number of seniors.

Data is for 2023-24 from the Department of Education and 2024-25 from the University of California. Schools with fewer than 30 seniors were not included. For instances where the number of applications was greater than the number of seniors, percentages were capped at 100%.  
Chart: Hanna Zakharenko/S.F. Chronicle · Source: [University of California](https://www.universityofcalifornia.edu/about-us/information-center/admissions-source-school), [California Department of Education](https://www.cde.ca.gov/ds/si/ps/)

In San Francisco, [Immaculate Conception Academy](https://www.icacristorey.org/) had the highest share of students who received admissions to a UC, with 70% of seniors admitted to a UC school. The all-girls school is in the [Cristo Rey Network](https://www.cristoreynetwork.org/about), a system of schools that operate as a work-study program for students from low-income families.

In Alameda County, [the Quarry Lane School](https://www.quarrylane.org/) in Dublin had the highest UC admission rate, at 90%. It is the [only private school](https://www.quarrylane.org/upper-school/international-baccalaureate-diploma-programme) in Alameda County to offer the international baccalaureate program. The very top school in California is [BASIS Independent Silicon Valley](https://basisindependent.com/schools/ca/silicon-valley/), a private school network that operates schools in [multiple states](https://basisindependent.com/our-locations/).

Just because a school gets more students into UCs doesn’t necessarily mean it’s doing a lot to help its students prepare for admission. Private schools that enroll larger shares of students from high-income and high-education families are likely to send more students to UC campuses regardless of school quality. The Chronicle [ranked public high schools](https://www.sfchronicle.com/projects/2026/best-public-california-high-schools/) on UC admission rates using a statistical analysis that accounted for these demographic differences. However, it was not possible to perform a similar analysis ranking private high schools this way because they are not required to submit demographic information to the California Department of Education. Still, the raw UC admission rates may be useful to parents and students.

The UCs reports systemwide admissions and data for individual schools. Because student-level admissions are not reported for each school, we do not know which campuses a student was admitted to. In place of this, we calculated the number of applications or admissions received for every 100 seniors at a given school. The table below shows admissions received per 100 seniors at competitive UCs, which include UC Berkeley, UCLA, UC Davis, UC Irvine, UC San Diego and UC Santa Barbara.

#### Admissions to competitive UCs per 100 seniors at California private high schools

Data for seniors who graduated in 2024. Numbers can include multiple applications and admissions for one student.

“Competitive UCs” include UCLA, UC Berkeley, UC Irvine, UC Davis, UC Santa Barbara and UC San Diego. Data is for 2023-24 from the Department of Education and 2024-25 from the University of California. Schools with fewer than 30 seniors were not included. University of California data is not available for schools with less than five applicants or three admittances, in which case applications or admissions per 100 are listed as "Unknown."  
Chart: Hanna Zakharenko/S.F. Chronicle · Source: [University of California](https://www.universityofcalifornia.edu/about-us/information-center/admissions-source-school), [California Department of Education](https://www.cde.ca.gov/ds/si/ps/)

The International School of San Francisco received 81 admissions for every 100 seniors, the most admissions into competitive UCs out of private schools in San Francisco. The school has one of the [pricier tuition price tags](https://www.sfchronicle.com/projects/2025/sf-bay-area-private-school-tuition/) for the city, but offers international baccalaureateI and French baccalaureate programs.
